Project Goals¶

This project explores the relationship between lifestyle behaviors and mental health outcomes. I am particularly interested in understanding how stress, anxiety, and treatment-seeking behaviors are influenced by sleep quality, academic environment, and general lifestyle factors.

To investigate these themes, I identified three potential datasets that examine mental health from different perspectives: (1) a Sleep Health and Lifestyle dataset, (2) a general Mental Health dataset, and (3) a Student Mental Health dataset. All three datasets share common themes such as stress levels, anxiety, and behavioral factors, which allows for comparative exploration and selection of the most analytically meaningful dataset for the final tutorial.

The goal of this project is to walk through the full data science pipeline while answering measurable questions about the relationship between stress and behavioral factors such as sleep quality, treatment, and course of study.

The main questions guiding this analysis are:

  1. Is there a measurable relationship between sleep quality and stress levels?

  2. Are individuals who report increasing stress also more likely to report difficulty coping?

  3. Does anxiety prevalence vary across students’ academic experience (e.g., year of study)?

These questions allow the project to explore mental health from multiple perspectives using different but related datasets.

The primary question I aim to investigate is: Is there a measurable relationship between sleep quality and stress level?

I selected this dataset for initial ETL and exploratory analysis because it contains clean numerical variables that are well-suited for statistical analysis and visualization.

# Compute and visualize correlations between lifestyle and sleep variables
corr_matrix = df[['sleep_duration','quality_of_sleep','stress_level','physical_activity_level','daily_steps','heart_rate']].corr()

plt.figure(figsize=(8,6))
sns.heatmap(corr_matrix, annot=True, cmap="coolwarm")
plt.title("Correlation Matrix of Sleep and Lifestyle Variables")
plt.show()

The heatmap shows the correlations between key sleep and lifestyle variables in the dataset. Sleep duration and sleep quality have a strong positive correlation (0.88), indicating that individuals who sleep longer tend to report better sleep quality. Both sleep duration and sleep quality show strong negative correlations with stress level (-0.81 and -0.90), suggesting that better sleep is associated with lower stress. Additionally, physical activity level is strongly correlated with daily steps (0.77), which reflects expected behavioral patterns. These relationships suggest that sleep and lifestyle behaviors are closely linked to stress levels.

Dataset 2: Mental Health Dataset¶

This dataset contains survey responses related to stress experiences, coping behaviors, and mental health indicators. Variables include measures such as growing stress, coping struggles, mood swings, family history of mental health conditions, and lifestyle-related behavioral changes.

This dataset is valuable because it allows for examination of how perceived stress relates to behavioral and psychological responses. Rather than focusing on treatment-seeking behavior, this analysis explores whether individuals reporting increased stress also report greater difficulty coping.

A primary question I aim to investigate is: Is growing stress associated with coping struggles?

This question examines whether individuals who perceive increasing stress also experience greater difficulty managing that stress. Identifying such patterns may highlight the relationship between stress perception and coping capacity.

Dataset 3: Student Mental Health Dataset¶

Because students face unique academic and social pressures, this dataset allows for targeted exploration of mental health patterns within an educational environment.

This dataset is particularly useful for examining how mental health indicators vary across academic disciplines and whether certain fields of study are associated with higher levels of anxiety or stress.

A primary question guiding this analysis is: Does anxiety prevalence differ across academic majors?

This question explores whether academic environment and field of study may be associated with different mental health experiences among students.

The correlation heatmap suggests strong relationships between sleep quality, sleep duration, and stress level, which makes this dataset a good candidate for predictive modeling. The goal of this model would be to determine whether sleep-related behaviors significantly explain variation in stress levels.
